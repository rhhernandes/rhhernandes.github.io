Research

How AI reshapes what societies know

I am a PhD candidate at Cambridge Digital Humanities, University of Cambridge (Selwyn College), supported by a Harding Distinguished Postgraduate Scholarship. My research examines how artificial intelligence reshapes journalism and information environments, and what that means for mediation: the people, institutions, and systems that stand between events and public understanding.

My work keeps technology platforms under scrutiny. That means both the information circulating on them and the ability of others to examine it.

I am a Student Fellow at the Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence, and completed the MPhil in Ethics of AI, Data, and Algorithms at Cambridge with Distinction and the Huw Price Prize. I combine computational approaches — algorithm audits, large-scale text analysis, computational grounded theory — with interviews and ethical analysis, informed by more than a decade in newsrooms.

Themes

What I work on

AI and journalism

How newsrooms adopt AI systems, what those systems do to editorial judgement and labour, and what responsible adoption looks like in practice.

Information integrity

How platforms and generative systems change what publics can reliably know.

Mediation

Who and what now stands between events and public understanding, as algorithmic systems take over work once done by human gatekeepers.
